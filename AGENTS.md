# Lanka Car Hunter — Agent Operational Guidelines

## Vehicle Deal Scouting Protocol

When scouting, evaluating, or recommending car deals:
1. **Search Full Price Spectrum**: Never apply artificial lower price clamps when scouting a model. Always locate the absolute lowest-priced car on the market (including distress sales and manual variants).
2. **Disaggregate Specs**: Clearly distinguish **Manual vs. Automatic** and **Japanese vs. Indian/Regional** specs.
3. **Compare Against the Floor**: Always present the cheapest listing in the country side-by-side with your recommendation, explaining the exact tradeoff in price, condition, liquidity, and profit.
4. **Transparent Flip Math**: Provide complete cost breakdowns (purchase, detailing, mechanical prep, fees, resale target, net profit, ROI).
5. **Practical Inspection Checklist**: Include Sri Lanka-specific inspection points (coastal rust, gearbox health, suspension, AC coil).
6. **Colour Arbitrage**: White and black carry a real price premium in the Sri Lankan market — most buyers default to them, so they sit at or above the make/model average. Off-colours (silver, grey, blue, red, etc.) typically trade 5-15% below white/black for identical spec, purely from lower demand, not condition. Since the site's market-average benchmark blends all colours together, don't take "near market price" or "X% below market" at face value for a non-white/black car — check whether it's still cheap *after* accounting for the normal colour discount. A real example: a 2011 Tucson in grey sold for Rs. 6,050,000 while the cheapest black one was ~Rs. 7,000,000 — a gap far wider than the normal colour discount, which made it a genuine underpriced flip, not just "cheap because it's grey." That kind of gap is the strongest deal signal available and is often missed by anyone comparing raw prices without adjusting for colour.
